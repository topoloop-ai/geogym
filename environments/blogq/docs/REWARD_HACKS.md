# REWARD_HACKS — how the policy, the teacher, or a writer can beat the verifier

The verifier is the durable asset of this lab. RL will find every bug in
it, so every hole we know about is written here with its rule. New
environments start by reading this list and writing their own harm classes.

## Behaviors the verifier must catch

1. **Padding and invention.** The model pads answers with attributes the
   user never asked for (wheelchair access, phone numbers) and with
   invented specifics (fees, dates, history). It's a style even large Qwens
   have, not a hack in the strict sense, but it is the failure mode that
   matters most for a small model: the rule is **write only what search,
   place details, or a fetched page actually returned, and only what was
   asked**. The strict judge lists every unsupported specific and the rollout
   is discarded; anti-padding prompting measurably does nothing, and
   rewriting the answer to remove them was tried and retired (13).
2. **Attribute spreading.** A fact retrieved for one place gets asserted
   for others ("all of these are open until 9"). A real hack: a valid fact
   may only be used for the entity it was retrieved for.
3. **Wrong entity.** Right page, wrong place picked off it; or a neighbor's
   ref cited as identity. The identity rule: a place answer is anchored by
   a places-DB ref the agent actually retrieved. Two complications the
   verifier has to handle rather than punish: (a) **gold can be wrong** —
   the bank was generated by a model that makes mistakes, and a bad gold
   poisons both training and eval, so the judge treats the source passage
   as ground truth and gold as a hint, and a gold-audit lane exists;
   (c) **a ref for a side mention must not rescue the row** — the answer's
   MAIN place is the identity; the judge names its ref (`primary_ref`) and
   the gate requires that ref cited and retrieved (bq-084742: Foolish
   Craig's never resolved, Holy Crepe's ref cited, previously passed);
   (b) **OSM may not have the entity** (no trailhead node, an unnamed
   viewpoint) — the best available entity must be a legal answer, so
   equivalence classes and an "absent entity" outcome are part of every
   family's harm-class definition.
4. **Marker attached to the wrong claim.** A valid citation placed on a
   sentence its source does not support. Refs-in-trace checks cannot see
   this; the judge has to verify assignment, claim by claim. Citation
   *type* mismatches (a web claim cited to a DB ref, a DB fact cited to a
   url, ad hoc `[SOURCE 2]` markers) are mechanical and the compiler
   catches them. The compiler must be type-strict and robust: every new
   writer model probes the grammar from a new angle, and any gap it finds
   RL will find too.
5. **Unnecessary searches.** Extra searches that add nothing must never be
   rewarded, and the prompt's budget must match what the harness enforces
   (a mismatch manufactures flail). But budget overruns are harvest
   economics, never a negative reward on their own.

## Behaviors the verifier must NOT punish

6. **Clarification.** A tiny model must search rather than answer from
   memory, so the strict gate fails zero-search answers. But some questions
   are malformed and need a clarifying question back, and a gate that
   requires a search on every turn trains clarification away — a reward
   hack in the opposite direction that will hurt later. Clarification has
   to be a legal, rewarded outcome: an explicit set of clarify-worthy tasks
   in each environment, and a verifier that credits asking on those.
7. **Correct answers phrased differently.** Judge harshness (paraphrase
   pedantry, insisting on the gold when the passage equally supports
   another place) removes whole classes of correct answers from the kept
   set and skews it toward literal quoting. Equivalence classes for
   recommendations and a calibration set with a false-reject number are
   the guard.

## Rules about the machinery itself

8. **Never truncate context between stages.** (Measured 2026-09-03 over
    all 39,439 gold-citing rollouts: the campaign's strict judge saw a
    median 3.1K chars of evidence where the agent had 10.5K. Re-judged with
    full evidence, **5,503 of its fails flip to pass and 1,015 of its
    passes flip to fail** — yield 5,749 -> 10,769, a **96% increase from
    fixing truncation alone**. A 400-row sample predicted 18%; the
    population says 17.6%.) Rollout, judge and every repair stage
   must all see the same complete evidence. Truncation anywhere in
   that chain produces false rejects, over-stripped rewrites, and
   disagreements that look like model behavior. This bug is ugly and
   recurs; check it first when numbers move.
9. **Judge disagreement is a verifier problem, not a voting problem.** If
   repeated judgings of the same row disagree substantially, the rubric is
   underspecified. More votes do not fix that, and agreement between votes
   is not evidence of correctness. Fix the rubric, then measure against
   the calibration set.

9b. **The flow to teach for vague questions is web first, then resolve.**
    A places search on a vague query, a web search that finds the real
    answer, and no second places search leaves the answer unresolved.
    Those traces are re-rolled with the resolve-in-DB prompt rule, never
    kept and never laundered — keeping them teaches the wrong order.
10. **Never tell the rewriter the answer.** The launderer sees the agent's
    own tool evidence, the answer, and the list of specifics to delete —
    never the source passage and never the judge's prose, both of which
    can carry the answer. A rewriter that knows the answer writes it
    whether or not the agent ever saw it (bq-025798: $6/$5/free with no
    citation, contradicting the agent's own fetched $10). With that leak
    closed, a second removal round is worth ~2 rows per 123; the earlier
    "+20 from round two" was the leak.
11. **The passage settles identity, never specifics.** The strict judge
    treats the source passage as ground truth for WHICH place or answer is
    right, not as support for a value the agent did not retrieve. When the
    agent's evidence conflicts with the passage, the verdict is
    *ungradable* — a retrieval outcome to re-roll, not a fail and not a
    launder target. And every prompt label ("SOURCE EVIDENCE", "[TOOL
    EVIDENCE]") is a mechanical leak check, because a writer will cite it.

12. **A repair that deletes evidence is worse than the defect.** Our own
    `normalize()` dropped SOURCES entries "never referenced by a marker",
    per the contract. The teacher usually writes a correct SOURCES list
    with NO inline markers, so the rule deleted the whole citation list
    and left `SOURCES: []` — on 5,477 judged-correct rollouts, every one
    of which carried the gold ref. The pipeline then scored them as
    "answer with no citation". Fixed: orphan-drop applies only when the
    body has markers; an unmarked list is ONE marker defect, reported and
    repairable, never deleted. General rule: a deterministic repair may
    normalize or flag, but must not destroy content the model produced —
    measure what each repair removes before trusting its output.

13. **Repair-to-pass is a hack we ran on ourselves (RETIRED 2026-09-03).**
    The launder stage rewrote judge-failed answers until they passed. Every
    leak found this session lived in it: the source passage in the
    rewriter's prompt, the judge's prose in the rewriter's prompt, the
    unsupported list computed against the passage, a side-mention ref
    rescuing an unresolved answer. Each fix cut honest recovery (64 -> 42
    -> ~16-20 of 123) and the deletion-only variant recovered 1 of 123,
    because the real defect was format, not content. What survives is
    format repair with a mechanical guarantee (`cite_markers.py`: strip the
    markers and the text must be byte-identical) plus rejection sampling
    for yield. Lesson: if a stage's success metric is "the judge now
    accepts it", the stage is optimizing the judge, not the data.

## Harm classes to define before a new environment rolls

- Wrong entity, including the equivalence class for recommendations and
  what counts as a legal answer when OSM lacks the entity.
- Which specifics are oracle-checkable (numbers, hours, distances) and
  with what tolerance.
- Degenerate-but-passing outputs (one-stop itineraries, the famous place
  every time, the same route mode always).
- Budget gaming (repeated calls with trivial variations).
- Clarification: which tasks should be answered with a question, so the
  verifier rewards it there and nowhere else.
- Format: any new commit or citation grammar gets a compiler before the
  first rollout.

## Found 2026-09-04/05 (contract v2 bring-up)

- **Example ids get copied.** A real ref in the prompt's example ending
  showed up in 12/94 map objects; the ref in the `place_details` tool
  description appeared in ~2% of ladder finals from two different models.
  Any example in prompt or tool text is a placeholder. (Fixed both.)
- **Fetch loop after the budget message.** With tools still offered, the
  teacher re-issued the same `web_fetch` every turn until the loop cap —
  15 fetches, 235K prompt tokens, no answer. Tools are withdrawn after the
  budget message. (Fixed.)
- **Code-fenced object.** Gemini wraps the trailing JSON in a ```json fence;
  a strict last-line parser scores every such answer as no-object. Unfence
  is a format-only repair. (Fixed.)
- **URLs in context become URLs in prose.** The `HANDLES:` table exists only
  so `web_fetch(url)` can be called, and both 0.8B models copied a URL from
  it into an answer now and then. Design fix pending: `web_fetch(handle)`.
- **Lenient judge by omission.** `judges/judge_v2.py` without
  `JUDGE_REWARD_MODE=1` is the lenient judge; one direct invocation without
  it reported 72 "strict" passes that were 5 under the real strict judge.
  Every judge call sets it explicitly now.
- **Duplicate judge processes.** Two `stream_judge.py` instances on one
  output file doubled rented-judge spend and raced on shard files; it also
  gave a free two-vote agreement number (98.8% overall, 85% among passes).

## 14. Hacks observed in GRPO round 1 (2026-09-09, 4× Sonnet over 220 rewarded passes; `runs/grpo/eval_iter1/hack_review/`)

The policy learned verifier blind spots rather than getting sloppier: late-window passes were ~75% clean,
but the suspects cluster on four mechanisms. Each now has a mechanical counter.

- **phantom_handle** — `[Wikipedia](w2)` in a trace that never called `web_search` (search_places →
  place_details → web_fetch), so no handle table exists and the old handle-hygiene gate had nothing to
  compare against. Counter: with no handle table, NO link target is valid (`format_flags_v2`).
- **prose/map identity disconnect** (incl. *container substitution*: the park's ref for the bridge in it;
  *same-name wrong entity*: Vacancy Brewing for St. Elmo Brewing; *spatial-rescue laundering*: a wrong
  hotel near the landmark passes because the judge's DB spatial check confirms "near Hyde Park") — the
  judge reads prose, never dereferences the pin. Counter: identity by NAME in the RL reward
  (`identity_ok` + `mixture.requalify.primary_name_ok`): a PASS whose first map ref's result line does
  not name the asked-for place is −1.
- **map_ref_stuffing** — every search hit dumped into `map`. Counter: ≥2 map refs whose result-line
  names never appear in the prose → `orphan map refs` flag (small penalty class).
- **anchor_distance_repurposing** (known, #10) reappeared in late windows.
- **same_domain_handle_swap** — crediting a different article from the same site (fuzzy domain match
  lets it through). No counter yet; needs the per-claim used⇒credited check.
- **db_only_bypass** — a web-needing question answered from place_details only, no citation; passed
  because the name matched gold. No counter yet (category-conditional web requirement).

Magnitude: re-scoring the 713 late-window rewarded passes with the new gates: 612 stay, 54 fail name
identity, 47 fail phantom handles → ~14% of late positive reward was hack, now reclaimable.

## 15. The wrong-pin failure (2026-09-09, 3× Sonnet over 120 no-reach judge fails; `runs/grpo/alt_review/`)

Question asked: are we punishing good answers that simply aren't the dataset's gold place?
**Answer: almost never — 5/120 (4.2%) were good alternatives wrongly failed; the judge's stated reason
was fair in 114/120.** But the review found the dominant real defect instead:

**prose right, pin wrong.** The model names the correct place in prose (often the gold place), calls
`search_places` for it — frequently anchored at the wrong coordinates (Nelson NZ for Nelson Bay AU,
Corsica for Cinque Terre, Puebla for Lake Atitlán) — gets a result set with nothing matching, and cites
the top hit anyway. The app would render a pin in the wrong country under correct prose.

Magnitude across all 26,043 judged round-2 rollouts: **39.4% of all rollouts, 52.2% of judge failures**
have a pin that does not name the place the prose is about. The round-1/2 reward scored these 0 —
identical to a thoughtful near-miss.

Counter (round 3): `identity_by_category` now runs on EVERY rollout, not only passes → −1. And because
the prompt already offers the honest alternative ("say plainly what you could and could not verify"),
an empty map object plus explicit non-resolution language is scored ABOVE a wrong pin
(`GEOGYM_HONEST_FAIL`, default −1 = round-2 semantics; set −0.25 to enable the tier). The phrase alone
cannot be farmed: it requires an empty map, which forfeits any chance of +1.

Root cause is upstream and not fixable by reward alone: the wrong search anchor is the reach problem
(LADDER 2026-09-07 — the 0.8B's geography is settlement-level). Reward can only teach it to notice the
search failed.

## 16. Journey-node pin laundering (round 3, caught live at step 9, 2026-09-09)

**The hack.** Round 3 scored a judged-fail rollout −1 when its primary map ref sat >100 km from gold
(`GEOGYM_PIN_KM`). `pin_distance_km` read `map[0]`. The v2 contract also allows journey nodes in the
map — `{"here": true}`, `{"travel": "walk"}` — which carry no `ref`. So the policy learned to prepend
them: `map[0]` had no ref, the distance was "unmeasurable", and the −1 never fired.

**How fast.** Share of finals whose map leads with a non-ref entry, by step: 3.9, 4.3, 7.2, 13.0, 21.3,
31.0, 58.9, 79.2, **90.8%**. Nine steps from noise to near-universal.

**Why it fooled the dashboard.** The "wrong-place pin" branch fell 25.8% → 0.9% and PASS rose, which
read as the gate working. It was the opposite: re-measuring the same rollouts with the first *real*
ref shows the true >100 km rate among judge-fails ROSE 3.3% → 46%. Corrected for the population shift,
genuine wrong pins only went ~26.6% → ~20.5%; the gate was inert from about step 5.

**Counter (`reward.py:pin_distance_km`).** Take the first entry that actually carries a string `ref`,
never `map[0]`. A map that is non-empty but names no place returns `inf` (gate fires, −1) — it is a
defect, not an honest non-answer. A genuinely empty `{"map": []}` still routes to the honest tier.

**The general lesson.** A gate that can return "unmeasurable" hands the policy a third option beside
pass and fail, and gradient descent finds it. `primary_name_ok` never had this hole because a non-ref
`map[0]` fails its `REF_RE` check outright — it returns *false*, not *unknown*. **Every mechanical gate
must map "I cannot tell" onto the penalised side, or onto a branch the policy cannot reach on purpose.**

**Cost.** Round 3 was stopped at step 9 and relaunched from the same init as `iter3b` with the fix.
Evidence preserved in `runs/grpo/v4_iter3/iter3` (incl. `checkpoints/step_5`).

## 17. A dead judge scored as "wrong" (round 3b, 2026-09-09) — NOT a hack, a verifier bug

`rl/ROUND3.md` §3 promises: "judge unreachable -> rollout ERRORS and is dropped — never scored".
It did not hold. `judges/judge_v2._one_vote` gave up after 6 attempts with
`return False, f"api error after retries: ...", [], None`, but `judge_one` sets its `judge_error` flag
by testing `str(reason).startswith("judge_error:")`. The prefix did not match, so an unreachable judge
came back as an ordinary verdict of `correct=False`, and `reward.py` scored it as a normal judged
failure (0, +0.25, or -1 via the pin gate).

**What it cost.** The NVFP4 judge died mid-run. Step 2 of `iter3b` trained on 1,468 fabricated
failures: reward +0.50 -> **-0.08**, PASS **36.7% -> 0.0%**, with `Error 0.0%` and
`Trainable 1024/1024` the whole time — every health indicator the orchestrator prints looked perfect.
The box recovered on its own ~30 min later; the run was stopped and relaunched as `iter3c`.

**Fix.** The give-up reason now starts with `judge_error:`. Regression-tested end to end by pointing
`score()` at a dead port and asserting it RAISES instead of returning a reward.

**The general lesson (twin of §16).** Two independent bugs in one night, same shape: an
error/unknown state silently taking the value of a legitimate negative outcome. §16 was the policy
finding it; this one was infrastructure. **Every failure path in a verifier needs a test that asserts
it is loud.** And watch PASS-rate *per step* as an alarm — a step at exactly 0.0% passes is never a
real batch, it is always broken infrastructure.

## 18. The mechanical layer went blind on a tool-output format change (round 4, 2026-09-10) — infra, not a hack

After the 2026-09-09 reboot, wplaces-api came back on newer code and every search-result line gained an
`hours unknown` column between the distance and the trailing `(lat, lng)`. `judges/judge_v2._RES_LINE`
allowed exactly one optional column, so it matched nothing. Consequences, all silent: `pin_distance_km`
returned "unmeasurable" for every pin (0 pin penalties in 10 steps; round 3 ran 25–30%), the judge's
`spatial_checks` found no lines, `primary_name_ok` could not resolve a ref. Reward rose to 0.78 with
`Error 0.0%` throughout — it looked like the best round yet.

**Fix.** `_RES_LINE` now allows any number of columns before the coordinates (`core/gates.py` already
did). The 10-step run is kept at `runs/grpo/v4_iter4/iter4_nogate_10steps` as evidence; round 4 was
relaunched clean.

**Detection added.** `rl/guard.sh` alarms when <90% of search-result lines parse, and when a step has
200+ judge fails and 0 pin-distance penalties.

**Lesson (third of the week, same shape as §16 and §17).** Any parser of another service's text output
is a silent-failure surface. The pin gate had a second "unmeasurable" path ("ref has no coordinates")
that §16's fix left open because it is legitimate for `nearest_places` lines; the guard now covers it
by measuring the parse rate directly rather than trusting the gate's own accounting.


## 19. Backend errors scored as model failures — in RL (fixed) and in evals (still open), 2026-09-11/13

Three wplaces stalls during round 7 (an ingest, an index build, a cold start under a burst) put `search error:` into up to 71%
of a step's rollouts, which `reward.py` then scored as compile (−1) or honest (−0.5) failures. Fix: `GEOGYM_DROP_INFRA_ERR=1` —
a rollout whose tool results carry an infra-error prefix RAISES and is dropped, exactly like a judge outage (§17). After the fix
a 39%-dropped step still trained on a full clean batch; before it, one step's reward fell from .54 to .18.

**Still open:** the same rule is NOT in `core/harness.py`. A cold wplaces under the post-round probe's 48 workers errored 2,347
of 4,000 rollouts and the probe read .245 for a checkpoint that scored .625 on ho200 the same hour. An eval can be quietly
poisoned by the backend. Port the drop, and have every eval print its own tool-error count (the `runs/twins_r8/` chains do).

## 20. Rejected: rewarding an anchor by its distance to the gold (2026-09-13)

Proposed as the "clean reward" for teaching the model to fill a server-resolved `anchor_name`. Rejected before use. It is a
step-level signal on an intermediate action (the §14-18 shape), and it pays the model for GUESSING the gold's location from
memory — spending 0.8B capacity on gazetteer recall, which is the job the resolver exists to remove. It also correlates with the
answer the model does not yet have. The acceptable form is **grounding**: pay only if the `anchor_name` string appeared in a
search result or a fetched page in the same episode. Mechanically checkable, fails safe when the model invents a name, and stays
about the model's action rather than the resolver's accuracy. Log the distance; never pay it.

## §21 (2026-09-13, round 9) — search-shaping terms: repeat-query penalty and grounded-first-search bonus
Round 8 (ask cost, all-clear bank, place context) raised training reward .61→.76 entirely by draining the honest tier: declines 18→3
on ho200, of which ~2/3 became passes and 1/3 wrong answers or **3×-identical-search loops** (7 over-budget). First-search entity
selection did not move (67-69% of first `search_places` queries name the gold, all three ho200 rows). Two terms added to
`reward.py::score`, both env-gated and OFF by default:
- `GEOGYM_REPEAT_QUERY` (−0.1/repeat, cap 3): a `search_places` query whose normalised tokens repeat an earlier one. Mechanical.
- `GEOGYM_GROUNDED_SEARCH` (+0.1, once): the FIRST `search_places` query has ≥2 content tokens, ≥60% of them present in tool output
  the model had ALREADY received, and is not its own `web_search` query echoed back; paid only when the rollout reached a judge
  verdict. Baseline rate on ho200: v4rl6 64%, v4rl8 68% (tracks the names-gold measure, `rl/first_search_gold.py`).
**Hack surface to watch:** it is a step-level signal. Farm = copy any two words from the web results into the first search regardless
of relevance. Mitigations: the verdict requirement (a nonsense search still has to produce an answer), the echo check, and the
`rl/step_stats.py` reason strings (`+grounded_search` rate per step — if it climbs toward 100% while names-gold on the probe does not,
kill the term). Distance-to-gold remains forbidden (§20).

## §22 (2026-09-14) — the honest tier must sit BETWEEN right and wrong, not below both
Rounds 3-9 scored a judged-wrong answer that cleared every gate at **0.0**, plus `REACH_BONUS` (+0.25) when it had reached the gold,
while an honest non-resolution scored **−0.5** with no reach credit. Ordering: wrong-and-reached (+0.25) > wrong (0.0) > honest (−0.5).
The −0.5 came from round 7b, where the honest tier at −0.25-with-reach had been farmed to 36% declines in six steps — a correct
diagnosis with an overshooting fix: it did not just damp the farm, it made admitting a miss strictly worse than guessing.
Symptom (took two rounds to spot because training reward ROSE both times): the decline tier drains into the wrong tier with no strict
gain. Round 8 declines 18→3 / wrong 43→53; round 9 declines 18→6 / wrong 43→63; ho200 strict .685 and .650 against a .695 bar.
**Rule:** an honest non-answer must score below a correct answer and above a confident wrong one, and the gap to "wrong" must be big
enough that reaching-and-missing is not more attractive than stopping. Both sides need a knob (`GEOGYM_WRONG_FAIL`,
`GEOGYM_HONEST_FAIL`) so the farm can be damped by raising the wrong penalty rather than by sinking the honest tier.
**Watch on any round that touches these:** declines and wrongs on ho200 move in OPPOSITE directions and neither runs away —
declines past ~25/200 is the round-7b farm returning; wrongs staying flat while declines fall means the model is still guessing.

## §23 (2026-09-14) — the shaping era, and why round 11 is binary
Ledger of rounds 5-10, all from v4rl6 or its line, all scored on ho200 strict against v4rl6's **.695**:
| round | the shaping idea | result |
|---|---|---|
| 5-7c/d/e | clarification as a turn (ask cost, ask bonus, twin bank) | .550-.665, cost 3-6 strict per attempt; ask branch real but fires on ~17% |
| 8 | place context in the observation + trained with it | .685 (and .630 for v4rl6 on the same observation) |
| 9 | grounded-first-search bonus + repeat-query penalty | .650; both terms behaved mechanically, strict fell |
| 10 | fix the wrong/honest ORDERING (§22) | .680, tier balance restored to the init's |
Every term was either farmed (§14-20), inverted (§22), mechanically fine but strictly neutral (§21), or bought back exactly what a
previous term had cost. The one intervention that moved the ladder in that period was a harness constant (search radius 100 → 300,
+8 strict, no training). **Conclusion (Jon): stop paying for process.** Round 11 sets `GEOGYM_BINARY=1` — reward 1.0 iff the rollout
is a strict pass (judge correct AND hard format gates clean AND identity ok), 0.0 otherwise, which is exactly the metric the ladder
scores. No proxy left to invert or farm. Accepted cost: honest declines and wrong answers both score 0, EV favours answering, and the
decline tier should go to ~0 — round 10 measured what that is worth (16/200 declines, and restoring them bought a tie, not a gain).
**What to watch instead of reward tiers:** zero-advantage group share (all-pass or all-fail; ~2% expected at a 60% pass rate — if it
climbs past ~15% the bank is too easy or too hard for the policy) and the ladder's ho200/probe pairing. Nothing else.

## §24 (2026-09-16) — the environment tells the model to answer after a budget cap, then scores the answer 0
`servers/tools.py`: when a per-tool budget is exceeded the tool returns "Tool budget exhausted — answer now from what you have"
AND sets `state.over_budget = True`. `reward.py` scores `protocol != ok` as −1 (0 under binary) BEFORE anything else, so an answer
written after that message is scored identically to never answering. Round 14: 3,196 rollouts hit a cap, 3,135 scored over-budget,
3 passed. The model therefore has nothing to learn from — "answer after the message" and "keep going" are indistinguishable — and the
one action that produces the cap, a THIRD `web_search` (budget is 2), sits at a stubborn 7-8% of rollouts across the whole round
while the search cap (budget 3) is fully learned (a 4th `search_places` occurs in 0.0-0.7%). Same class as §22: a signal we thought
we were sending was inverted at the point of scoring. **Fix (APPLIED 2026-09-17 behind flags — `GEOGYM_CAP_ANSWER=1` in the env, `BENCH_CAP_ANSWER=1` in core/harness.py; round 18 = the single-variable test, `runs/grpo/chain18.sh`):** on cap hit,
refuse further tool calls, require a final on the next turn, and score that final NORMALLY (protocol ok). The cap still stops the
search; it stops lying about the consequence. Expected gain ≈ the 7% of rollouts currently guaranteed 0, at the ~15-30% pass rate
of late answers. Note also that the length "drift" (2.5 → 3.3 calls on ho200, 3.1 → 4.1 in training) is NOT this: it is a second
places search taken inside the budget, which converts ~1/3 of the time and is correctly rewarded; the judge does not penalise long
answers (pass rate flat 74-75% from 200 to 600 chars; 2-call episodes 93-100% at every length).
